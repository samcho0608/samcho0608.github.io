# English Blog Style

Apply this for English posts in `content/`.

## Core style

Write in English that feels:
- natural
- direct
- grounded
- calm
- specific
- thoughtful without sounding performative

Do not make the prose sound:
- translated
- corporate
- devrel-like
- over-polished
- like generic AI blog writing

## Readability

Prioritize:
- natural paragraph flow
- clear transitions
- readable pacing
- headings only when useful

A good post should feel readable first and structured second.

## Voice cues

The writing often works well when it:
- gets to the point reasonably early
- narrows toward what actually mattered
- contrasts expectation and reality
- makes judgments clearly
- stays honest about uncertainty
- avoids pretending that every piece has a neat takeaway

Useful movements include:
- “What mattered more was ...”
- “At first, this looked like ... but the real issue was ...”
- “In theory, that sounds reasonable. In practice, ...”
- “The problem was not X. It was Y.”
- “I did not think this would matter much, but it did.”
- “It was not exactly wrong, but it was not the point either.”

These are examples, not required phrases.

## Technical writing

When the topic is technical:
- stay close to real engineering judgment
- explain actual constraints
- explain trade-offs honestly
- distinguish theory from production reality
- avoid vague claims
- include detail only when it helps understanding

Useful details may include:
- scale
- latency
- failure modes
- retry behavior
- idempotency
- observability
- rollback concerns
- coupling
- maintenance cost
- complexity trade-offs

Avoid phrases like:
- robust and scalable
- seamless
- production-ready
- best practice
- optimized

unless they are explained concretely.

## Non-technical writing

If the topic is personal, reflective, cultural, or about music:
- do not force a technical voice onto it
- do not turn it into self-help writing
- do not try to sound profound
- let the prose breathe
- preserve specificity and restraint

A post can simply preserve:
- a thought
- a change in judgment
- a personal preference
- an observation worth keeping
- an experience that stayed with me

That is enough.
